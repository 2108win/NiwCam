# NiwCam: background matting and face detection research (2026-11-22)

Scope: research only, no code changed. Every claim has a URL; "not published" / "not verified" means the page I could read did not say. Anything without a primary source is labelled "engineering practice". Licence rule used: GPL/AGPL/CC-NC/"non-commercial research" = unusable. Sources fetched on 2026-11-22; Hugging Face, ort.pyke.io and Kaggle pages sometimes refused (401/403), so some items stay "not verified".

## 0. Where we are today (from our own code)

- Segmenter (`src/engine/Segmenter.h/.cpp`): PP-HumanSeg 2023mar, 192x192 square input, Windows ML (`DirectXHighPerformance`, CPU fallback), ~15 inferences/s on its own thread. The mask is averaged with a fixed `0.5 * (old + new)` (Segmenter.cpp ~l.176-178), then a linear ramp `(p - 0.3) / 0.4` clamped to 0..1 (~l.183) turns it into a near-binary mask. `BlendBackground` upsamples the 192x192 mask bilinearly to half picture size and blends on NV12 with a blur made from a box-averaged tiny copy (blocks 8..40 px).
- Consequences (my reading, to be confirmed by measurement): the edge is a hard ramp 1 cell wide (1080/192 = 5.6 px per mask cell at 1080p, 10 px at 1920 wide); a mask up to 66 ms old is applied to a newer frame; a square 192x192 squeezes 16:9 video (the model sees an anamorphic picture); no hair detail at all; the fixed EMA ghosts on fast motion and does not fix flicker on uncertain pixels.
- Faces (`FaceDetect.h`): `Windows.Media.FaceAnalysis.FaceTracker`, grey copy up to 640 px long side, one detection every 200 ms; `AutoFrame.h` `ComposeShot` frames "about three face heights tall"; skin smoothing reuses FaceTracker boxes (5/s).

### What RVM's own tables say (the only like-for-like numbers; RVM paper, [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.11515))

| Test (low res) | MODNet MAD / Grad / dtSSD | RVM MAD / Grad / dtSSD |
|---|---|---|
| VideoMatte240K 512x288 | 9.41 / 1.89 / 2.23 | 6.08 / 0.88 / 1.36 |
| Distinctions-646 512x512 | 10.62 / 3.35 / 1.57 | 7.28 / 2.81 / 1.01 |
| AIM 512x512 | 21.66 / 5.37 / 1.76 | 14.84 / 4.35 / 1.01 |

Also from Table 2: the paper's own HD baseline is "MODNet + FGF" (Fast Guided Filter) on VideoMatte240K 1920x1080: SAD 11.13, Grad 15.30, dtSSD 3.08 (RVM: 6.57 / 10.55 / 1.90). So MODNet + a guided-filter upsample is the published permissive reference, about 1.5-1.7x worse than RVM on these sets. No published comparison of PP-HumanSeg vs MODNet vs MediaPipe on the same set was found: measure it (section 6).

### Reading of the table

1. Soft alpha beats a 2-class mask for hair and motion blur; MODNet is the only permissive soft-alpha, ONNX, webcam-oriented model found. Its price: 25 MB and a larger working size (paper numbers at 512x512), i.e. much more compute than our 192x192 PP-HumanSeg.
2. For weak PCs the landscape 16:9 inputs (MediaPipe 144x256, PP-HumanSegV2-Lite 256x144) are the best fit to a 16:9 webcam and the cheapest (64 MFLOPs for the Meet variant).
3. RVM stays a benchmark only. We must not ship, link or port its code or weights.

## 2. Techniques around the model

| Technique | What / why | Primary source | Notes for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft alpha edges | Keep alpha continuous; do not threshold. A hard `0.3..0.7` ramp (our `Segmenter.cpp` ~l.183) removes exactly the information a matte model provides | MODNet / RVM output alpha [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11961) | with a 2-class model, keep the raw probability and let the guided filter shape the edge |
| Uncertainty-weighted temporal blend | Per pixel: `out = new + (prev - new) * uncertainty(new) * ratio`, uncertainty ~1 at p=0.5, ~0 at p=0 or 1 (fifth-order polynomial in `(p-0.5)^2`). Confident pixels follow the new frame at once (no ghost trail), ambiguous edge pixels settle | MediaPipe [`segmentation_smoothing_calculator.cc`](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/mediapipe/calculators/image/segmentation_smoothing_calculator.cc) | replaces our fixed 0.5 EMA (Segmenter.cpp ~l.176-178). Zero added latency |
| Recurrent state | RVM's ConvGRU (h0 = zeros) gives temporal coherence inside the model | [RVM paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.11515) | not available in a permissive model (section 1) |
| One-Frame Delay | flicker pixel = frame t differs from t-1 and t+1 which agree; replace by their mean | MODNet paper [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.11961) | costs one frame of latency (33 ms at 30 fps): offer only in "best quality" |
| Optical-flow / DIS post-processing | PaddleSeg reduces video jitter with `--use_optic_flow` (DIS method) | [PaddleSeg](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleSeg/tree/release/2.9/contrib/PP-HumanSeg) | CPU cost unpublished; skip unless the cheap blend is not enough |
| Guided filter against the full-res frame | Edge-aware, snaps the soft low-res mask to image edges; O(N), and the fast version runs at subsampling ratio s in O(N/s^2) with ">10x speedup, almost no visible loss" | He & Sun, Fast Guided Filter [arXiv 1505.00996](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.00996) (original He/Sun/Tang paper not fetched) | the standard fix for our hard, blocky mask: run it on **luma at 1/4 resolution** (NV12 gives luma for free), radius ~ 2-4 mask cells at that scale, small eps. OpenCV exposes it as `ximgproc` guidedFilter / fastGuidedFilter (docs page refused fetch, not verified) |
| Joint bilateral filter on the mask | Google Meet smooths the low-res mask with a JBF, then an optional refinement aligns it to image edges; skipped on low-end devices | [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/background-features-in-google-meet-powered-by-web-ml/) | alternative to the guided filter; the guided filter is cheaper |
| Low-res inference then upsample | Meet: 256x144 (64 MFLOPs) or 160x96 (27 MFLOPs) chosen by hardware, then the mask is upsampled. RVM: choose `downsample_ratio` so the working size is 256-512 | [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/background-features-in-google-meet-powered-by-web-ml/), [RVM doc](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/documentation/inference.md) | two quality tiers (section 5) |
| Blur: bokeh-like, no halos | Meet: separable circle-of-confusion-weighted filters (avoid halos), blur strength scaled per pixel by the mask value (simulates bokeh), blurred at low resolution then blended with the full-res frame | [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/background-features-in-google-meet-powered-by-web-ml/) | our box-averaged tiny copy is the same idea; the missing parts are the CoC-weighting (do not sample foreground colour into the background blur, otherwise a bright halo of person colour appears) and a disc (not Gaussian) kernel. Disc vs Gaussian preference: no primary source found beyond Meet's "simulates bokeh" |
| Blur depth cue / strength by depth | A real depth map would scale blur with distance | no primary source evaluated. Depth models (e.g. Depth Anything) were not researched here | not recommended: cost, and the mask already gives a person/background split |
| Colour decontamination at edges | Estimate the true foreground colour from image + alpha so the old background colour does not bleed into the edge of a replaced background | Germer et al., Fast Multi-Level Foreground Estimation [arXiv 2006.14970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14970) (abstract does not discuss edge bleeding explicitly; the method is foreground estimation from alpha, faster than prior methods) | only matters for "replace with image", not for blur (blur keeps the original pixels under the person). Cheap approximation: erode alpha 1 px for the foreground colour source (engineering practice, no source) |
| Light wrap | Meet's background replacement uses light wrapping to soften edges and reduce contrast halos | [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/background-features-in-google-meet-powered-by-web-ml/) | replace mode only |
| Dilate / feather | Slightly feathering helps hide mask error | engineering practice, no primary source found | superseded by guided filter |
| Blur in linear light | Averaging in gamma space darkens bright blobs | engineering practice, no primary source found for webcam effects | NV12 BT.601/709 gamma; try a cheap square/sqrt trick on luma only, judge by eye; low priority |
| Skip inference when the scene is static | A frame-difference gate on the small model input; keep the previous mask while the picture barely changes | engineering practice, no primary source found. (MediaPipe LIVE_STREAM drops frames while the model is busy: [docs](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/image_segmenter)) | keeps CPU/GPU idle; must still run now and then (light change). Our code already runs at <= 15 Hz |
| Frame pacing | Apply the newest mask to the *current* frame and re-snap with the guided filter, instead of showing a stale mask | engineering practice, no primary source; project notes on QPC pacing in CLAUDE.md | the guided filter on the current frame hides most of the 0-66 ms staleness |
| Zoom / Teams internals | no public documentation found | - | do not claim anything about them |

**Framing verdict.** For one or two people at a desk, a face box (YuNet at 5-10 Hz) plus a Kalman filter is enough; the current rule (about 3 face heights tall) already encodes head-and-shoulders. A person/pose model adds value only when the face is turned away or hidden, or to centre on the shoulders. The cheapest route is not to add a detector: crop around the tracked face (the top-down input RTMPose wants, 256x192) and run RTMPose-t (3.2 ms CPU, published) at 2-5 Hz only while framing is on. That is a design suggestion; nothing here measures it on our frames.

**Version conflict to settle in the first build (unverified):** `ort` 2.0.0-rc.15 targets ORT 1.31 and enables the whole `api-17`..`api-31` chain by default, while the newest DirectML-enabled ORT package I found is `Microsoft.ML.OnnxRuntime.DirectML` 1.24.4. The docs.rs features page does not say whether a DLL older than the compiled `api-N` level loads. Using DirectML probably means `default-features = false` and an `api-N` no higher than the DLL's version (or an older `ort` release matching it). **Third option:** the new Windows ML is ORT with "same ONNX APIs" and ships DirectML (legacy), so `ort` could load Windows ML's copy via `load-dynamic`/`init_from` (same Windows App SDK / unpackaged-exe caveat as above; not verified).

Suggestions (mine, to be measured): `ort` with `load-dynamic` + a pinned, DirectML-enabled ORT DLL next to the exe (version matched to the `api-N` level, see above), so the tray process never maps it; session created only when an effect is on and an app uses the camera (`ConsumerActive`); destroy it after ~30 s idle. DirectML stays mapped after use (our measurement), so the only way to guarantee the 24 MB tray figure after an effects session is a **helper process** that does the inference and exits when idle (process boundary costs one shared-memory copy of a 512x288 picture, small). Pin fixed input shapes (DML penalises dynamic dims). Because DirectML is in maintenance and WinML is the "Windows-supported" path, keep the model loading behind one trait so the backend can change.

## 5. Recommended pipelines

### A. "Best quality" (GPU PC)

1. Model: **MODNet webcam ONNX** (Apache-2.0, 25 MB) at **512x288** (16:9, multiples of 32; paper numbers are at 512x512), fixed shape, FP16 if DirectML accepts it (test). Run at the camera rate if the GPU takes <= 8 ms, else 15-30 Hz. Run the operator check first: ORT DML graph partitioning log must show no CPU fallback on the hot path.
2. Temporal: MediaPipe-style uncertainty-weighted blend of the new alpha with the previous one (ratio ~0.5-0.7, to tune). Optional OFD (+1 frame latency) behind a "smoothest" switch.
3. Upsample + edge snap: fast guided filter on 1/4-res luma of the **current** frame (r ~ 2-4, eps ~ 1e-3..1e-2 on 0..1 luma, to tune), bilinear to full size.
4. Blur: low-res disc-kernel blur with CoC/mask-weighted taps (no person colour in the blur), blur strength scaled by (1 - alpha), blended at full res with the current frame. Gamma-space first, linear-light variant only if it looks better.
5. Replace mode: foreground colour estimation (Germer) or 1 px erosion for the colour source, plus a light-wrap pass.
6. Gate: skip a model run when the 192-ish luma difference of the input is below a threshold and the last run is < 1 s old.

### B. "Balanced" (weak PC / no GPU)

1. Model: **MediaPipe Selfie landscape 144x256** (converted by us, 250 KB) or **PP-HumanSegV2-Lite 256x144**, CPU EP, 10-15 Hz. Published CPU cost: 8.3 ms for the Meet 256x144 model in WASM (2018 laptop).
2. Temporal: same uncertainty-weighted blend, no OFD.
3. Edge: fast guided filter at 1/8 luma resolution.
4. Blur: the current tiny-copy box blur + mask-weighted blend; no light-wrap.
5. Quality tier chosen from measured ms/frame on first use.

### C. Current model, free improvements (no new weights)

Raw probability instead of the 0.3-0.7 ramp (keep a mild contrast curve), uncertainty-weighted blend instead of the fixed EMA, a guided filter against the current luma frame, and test a letterboxed (not squeezed) 16:9 input to the 192x192 model. These alone address the hard, stale edge; their value is measurable with section 6.

### Face side

Replace FaceTracker with **YuNet `2026may`** (MIT, dynamic input) on the grey/RGB 320-640 px copy at 5-10 Hz, per-face constant-velocity Kalman on (cx, cy, size), ByteTrack-style second-chance matching for low-score boxes, and keep FaceTracker as the fallback when the model fails to load. Optional: RTMPose-t on the face-derived crop for shoulders. For skin smoothing, the Face Landmarker mesh would give eye/lip/brow exclusion (licence still to verify); until then YuNet boxes + the segmentation mask are enough.

## 6. How to measure (objectively)

Quality (needs ground truth):
- **MAD/SAD, MSE, Gradient, Connectivity**: definitions from Rhemann et al. 2009, the alphamatting.com benchmark [overview](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=157344), [supplement](https://hci.iwr.uni-heidelberg.de/vislearn/HTML/publications/papers/2009/cvpr09-matting-Eval_supMat.pdf). Their finding: method rankings change with the error function, so report all of them.
- **dtSSD** (temporal coherence): Erofeev et al., BMVC 2015 [paper](https://bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2015/papers/paper099/index.html); RVM reports it scaled by 1e2. The benchmark paper was written because no accepted temporal metric existed (and, per a search summary, finds temporal coherence matters more to viewers than accuracy; read the paper before quoting).
- **Datasets**: VideoMatte240K test composites (1920x1080 and 512x288 archives) from the BackgroundMattingV2 project [RVM training doc](https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/blob/master/documentation/training.md): **no licence stated on the pages I could read; read the dataset page agreement before using or redistributing**. P3M-10k / P3M-500-P (500 face-blurred portraits) and P3M-500-NP (500 public celebrity images) under a release agreement labelled "MIT License" [repo](https://github.com/JizhiziLi/P3M); AIM-500 (100 portraits among 500 images) under a similar agreement [repo](https://github.com/JizhiziLi/AIM); PPM-100 from the MODNet paper [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11961) (released with the code; licence not read). Distinctions-646 not researched. Use these for offline evaluation only; do not ship them.

Smoothness (no ground truth needed):
- **Flicker metric** (my definition, engineering practice): on a 20-60 s clip of a still sitting person recorded on our own webcam at 1080p30, mean absolute difference of alpha between consecutive frames, reported over (a) the 12 px edge band and (b) the rest of the picture; also on the composited output luma. Lower is better; compare old vs new pipeline on the same clip. Add clips: hair/hand motion, person leaves/enters, lighting change, a poster or TV showing a face, two people.
- Record our own clips so there is no licence question; if public clips are used, record the licence of each and use only ones that allow research use.
- **Edge stability proxy**: standard deviation over time of the edge position along 5 fixed scanlines (px).

Cost (this PC, with the app as it will ship):
- ms/frame p50/p95/p99 for model, post-processing and blend, from QPC around each stage; GPU vs CPU EP; first-inference (cold start) time and time-to-first-mask after "effect on".
- Process CPU %, GPU %, working set and private bytes: idle tray (target ~1 % CPU, ~24 MB private), effect on with an app reading 1080p30 and 1080p60, effect on with the window hidden, after turning the effect off (does memory go back; DirectML stays mapped in our measurement).
- Frames dropped / frame-interval max in `niwcam-verify --hold` while the effect is on (existing tool, the earlier measurement was "gaps <= 35 ms, 0 no-signal").
- A/B grid: {PP-HumanSeg current, MODNet 512x288, MediaPipe landscape, PP-HumanSegV2-Lite} x {as-is, + temporal blend, + guided filter} on the same clips and sets.
